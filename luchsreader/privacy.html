Privacy policy

Luchsreader for Android, Wear OS and Apple devices · last updated 7 November 2026

Nothing you do in Luchsreader reaches me. There's no Luchsreader server and no account. The app contains no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking. What you read, highlight and save stays on your devices, and, if you turn on sync, in your own Google Drive, encrypted so that only your devices can read it.

The app does talk to other servers: the publications, to fetch the news, and some of Google's services. They are listed below, with what each one sees.

What stays on your device

The lists and articles the app fetches, your read marks, saved stories, highlights and notes, reading places, chosen sections, settings, the article summaries you asked for, and the searchable archive of the articles you've opened are stored in the app's own storage on your device.

  • Reading aloud (Listen) happens on the device, with the system's voice or a voice model you download once. Nothing you listen to is sent anywhere.
  • Searching your archive happens on the device.
  • Keys and sign-ins. The app reads the Guardian with an API key: one built into the app, or your own if you add one. That key, your own Gemini and Claude (Anthropic) keys if you add them, your Stratechery feed address, your Walliser Bote sign-in token, the session cookies from signing in to the FT, the Economist, The Athletic or the NZZ, and the sync key are kept in the device's secure storage (on Android, files encrypted with an Android Keystore key; on Apple devices, the Keychain), never in a backup.
  • Android backup. Android's own backup to your Google account includes Luchsreader's database (read marks, saved stories, highlights and notes, sections and the archive) only when that backup is end-to-end encrypted; otherwise Luchsreader leaves it out. Moving to a new phone with a cable or Wi-Fi transfer copies it too. The watch app is never backed up.

Sync through your own Google Drive (optional)

Sync is off until you turn it on. Then you sign in to Google, and the app asks for one permission: its own hidden app-data folder in your Google Drive (drive.appdata). It cannot see, open or change any other file in your Drive.

  • Every file is encrypted on your device before it's uploaded (AES-256-GCM), with a key made on your device that only your devices have. The key never goes to Drive, so neither Google nor I can read the files. Files are padded so their size doesn't show how much you've read, and their names don't reveal the device or the day.
  • Inside the encryption are your read marks, saved stories, highlights and notes, reading places, chosen sections and synced settings, the summaries you asked for (their points and suggested questions, which model wrote them; a summary goes after 60 days unless the story is saved), the text of your archive, the name of each of your devices (as set in its settings, so you can tell them apart), and the keys and sign-ins above (so a new device is signed in too). Your Walliser Bote password is never stored or synced: it is used once to sign in, and only the token that comes back is kept.
  • You add a device by scanning a QR code or typing a recovery code. Keep the recovery code safe: without it, a new device can't read your files.
  • If the app ever finds an unencrypted file in its folder, it deletes it.
  • Start over makes a new key and deletes every Luchsreader file in the folder. Turning sync off stops that device syncing. To delete the folder yourself, open Google Drive on the web, then Settings › Manage apps › Luchsreader › Options › Delete hidden app data.

Luchsreader's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The app uses Drive access only to store and read its own sync files for you, and never shows, sells or transfers it to anyone.

Who the app talks to

None of these servers is mine. Like any website, each one sees the request, including your IP address and the app's name.

The publications

  • The Guardian: its Content API (content.guardianapis.com), with the API key, for lists, articles and searches; or its RSS feeds when there's no key or the day's quota is used up. Searching the Guardian's whole archive sends your search words to the Guardian.
  • The Verge (theverge.com), Daring Fireball (daringfireball.net), MacRumors (macrumors.com), Six Colors (feedpress.me, sixcolors.com), SRF (srf.ch), the NZZ (nzz.ch), The Economist (economist.com), the Financial Times (ft.com) and The Athletic (nytimes.com): their feeds and article pages.
  • Stratechery: your own subscriber feed, at the address you paste in Settings.
  • Signing in to the FT, the Economist, The Athletic or the NZZ (optional): you sign in on the paper's own page, shown inside the app. The app keeps the cookies that page sets and sends them back to that paper (only to it) when it fetches an article, so you get the full text your subscription allows. If a page refuses the app's request, the app loads it once more in a hidden in-app browser, with the same cookies, from that paper's site. Sign out in Settings deletes them.
  • Walliser Bote (optional): your email address and password go to the paper's sign-in service (identitytoolkit.googleapis.com and securetoken.googleapis.com, Google's Firebase, which the paper uses), and its articles come from the paper's database (firestore.googleapis.com) and pomona.ch.
  • Pictures come from the servers the articles name, such as the Guardian's media.guim.co.uk. The watch fetches pictures too; it gets everything else from your phone.
  • The All feed puts these papers' lists together on your device; it talks to no server of its own.

Links you open in an article open in your browser, under that site's own policy.

Finance

The Finance page and widget fetch public prices: from Yahoo Finance (query1.finance.yahoo.com) for markets, and the European Central Bank's daily reference rates through Frankfurter (api.frankfurter.dev). They send only which prices they want; nothing about you, and your watchlist stays on your devices.

Translation, summaries and questions (AI)

Settings › AI chooses who does each job: the model on your device, Claude (Anthropic) with your own key, or Gemini (Google) with your own key. Nothing is sent to either until you add a key, and then only when you tap Summarise, ask a question, or translate an article (with the DE ↔ EN toggle, or as it opens when German articles open in English) with that model chosen. The first time you use each of them, the app says what it will send and to whom, and sends nothing unless you tap Send (you can turn this off in Settings › AI). A summary is kept with the article and synced (above); your questions and their answers stay on the device only while the article is open, and go when you close the box.

  • SRF, NZZ and Walliser Bote articles can be read in English. If you add your own Gemini API key in Settings › AI (it syncs to your other devices with the other keys), the article's text, with the matching terms from your Swiss glossary, is sent to Google's Gemini API (generativelanguage.googleapis.com) under your key and Google's terms for it. This is so on Android and on Apple devices. If you choose Gemini Flash for summaries or questions, tapping Summarise or asking sends the article's text (and your question, with the earlier questions and answers about it) there in the same way. Nothing else about you is sent.
  • Claude (optional): if you add your own Anthropic API key in Settings › AI (it syncs to your other devices with the other keys), then when you tap Summarise, ask a question about an article, or translate one with a Claude model chosen, that article's text (and your question, and earlier questions and answers about it) is sent to Anthropic's API (api.anthropic.com) under your key and Anthropic's terms for it, and the answer comes back. Saving the key checks it with Anthropic, which sends the key and nothing else. Subscriber-only articles (FT, the Economist, the NZZ, The Athletic, the Walliser Bote) are sent too unless you turn off "Send subscriber-only text". Nothing else about you is sent. The app counts, on the device, how many tokens it sent each model this month; that count isn't sent anywhere.
  • Without a key, or with the model on the device chosen, the work is done on the device and the text isn't sent: on Android by Gemini Nano, part of Android, which the app reaches through Google's ML Kit (it may send Google the same kind of diagnostic information as the code scanner, below); on Apple devices by Apple's on-device Foundation Models.

Google (Android)

  • Google sign-in and Google Drive, only if you turn on sync, as described above.
  • Google's code scanner reads the sync QR code when you add a device. It runs in Google Play services, the camera picture stays on the phone, and Luchsreader itself never gets camera access. It is part of Google's ML Kit, which sends Google diagnostic information (device and app details, an installation identifier, performance figures and error codes) to keep the service working. See Google's ML Kit data disclosure. Typing the recovery code instead avoids the scanner.
  • Wear OS: the phone sends your watch the latest headlines and short stories, and the watch sends back Save and Open on phone, through Google's Wear OS connection in Google Play services. Google can relay this through its servers when the watch isn't near the phone.

Voices

On Android, if you choose the natural voice, its model (about 350 MB) is downloaded once from the sherpa-onnx project's releases on GitHub (github.com). GitHub sees the download, as with any file.

A build of the app (Android or Apple) made with a Google Cloud key can also offer a cloud voice. Only when you choose it, the text being read is sent to Google Cloud Text-to-Speech (texttospeech.googleapis.com); nothing else is (no account, no article or position).

Apple devices

The Apple apps include no analytics, advertising or other third-party SDK; they reach the services above (the publications, Google sign-in and Drive, Gemini and Claude with your keys, and the Walliser Bote's Firebase sign-in and database) directly over HTTPS. Google sign-in uses the system's web sign-in sheet. Voices for reading aloud, background refresh, widgets and the Apple Watch connection are parts of Apple's system, under Apple's privacy policy.

Permissions on Android

  • Internet and network state: to fetch the news, pictures, prices and the voice model, to translate, summarise and answer with your keys, and to sync.
  • Notifications: only for Listen's playback controls.
  • Foreground service (media playback) and keeping the device awake: so an article you're listening to carries on with the screen off.

Luchsreader doesn't ask for your location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos or files, and doesn't use the advertising ID.

Things you choose to send

  • Sharing a link, a quote or a PDF sends what you share to the app you pick, and nothing else.
  • Exporting highlights as Markdown or an article as a PDF makes a file you choose what to do with.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stores on the device. Sync files are deleted with Start over, or from Google Drive as above. I hold nothing about you, so there's nothing for me to delete.

Children

Luchsreader is meant for adults and isn't aimed at children.
